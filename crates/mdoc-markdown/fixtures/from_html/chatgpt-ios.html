This is a short services agreement for office redesign, apparently governed by a simple arrangement between a corporate client and an individual designer operating under a trading name.

From a legal and commercial perspective, it is very incomplete. I would not recommend signing or relying on it in its current form.

1. Parties and legal structure

  • Client: ORG_3, at ADDRESS_1.
  • Designer: PERSON_4, trading as ORG_4, at ADDRESS_2.
  • Client’s representative: PERSON_5, facilities manager.

The Designer appears to be an individual sole trader rather than a separately incorporated company. This distinction matters: PERSON_4 would generally be personally responsible for contractual obligations.

3. Particularly problematic provisions

A. Subjective acceptance mechanism

“The Client may withhold the second part until PERSON_5 signs off the work.”

This gives the Client considerable discretion over payment.

There is no definition of:

  • What constitutes satisfactory completion.
  • Whether sign-off can be unreasonably withheld.
  • How quickly PERSON_5 must inspect the work.
  • What happens if PERSON_5 does not respond.
  • Whether defects must be remedied before payment.

A better mechanism would establish objective acceptance criteria and a fixed inspection period.

B. Missing scope and deliverables

The agreement does not specify whether the Designer is responsible for:

  • Space planning and architectural drawings.
  • Interior design and furniture selection.
  • Procurement of furniture and materials.
  • Contractor coordination.
  • Construction or installation supervision.
  • Building regulations and compliance.
  • Final delivery and handover.

This creates substantial potential for disagreement.

C. Missing payment protection

The agreement does not explain:

  • Whether the deposit is refundable.
  • What happens if the project is cancelled.
  • Whether additional work requires written approval.
  • Whether invoices are required.
  • What happens if payment is delayed.

4. Potential legal and compliance considerations

Depending on the nature of the work and the parties’ location, additional issues may arise:

  • Professional indemnity insurance.
  • Public liability insurance.
  • Health and safety responsibilities.
  • Building permissions and regulatory compliance.
  • Intellectual property ownership of design drawings.
  • Confidentiality and access to office premises.
  • Responsibility for subcontractors.

If this is an English-law agreement, the absence of an express governing-law clause does not automatically make the agreement invalid, but it introduces unnecessary uncertainty.

5. What I would request before signing

At minimum:

  • Full description of the services and deliverables.
  • Project timeline and completion date.
  • Detailed fee breakdown and payment milestones.
  • Objective acceptance procedure.
  • Deposit refund and cancellation provisions.
  • Procedure for approving additional work and expenses.
  • Liability and insurance provisions.
  • Intellectual property ownership and usage rights.
  • Termination and dispute-resolution provisions.
  • Governing law and jurisdiction.

Overall assessment

High contractual uncertainty

The document establishes the basic commercial relationship, but it does not adequately regulate the actual project.

It is particularly weak in three areas: scope of work, acceptance/payment conditions, and allocation of liability.

It may be sufficient as an informal record of an arrangement between parties who already trust each other, but it offers limited protection if a disagreement arises.

One important qualification: this assessment is based only on the supplied text. The original agreement may contain additional pages, schedules or referenced documents.
